# First playable: turret approach

Accepted scope from the product owner's 2 November 2026 request. REQ-025, TC-029; partial MS-001, MS-005 and MS-008. This is a small runnable development slice, not M0/M1 acceptance of the whole game.

Start in a protected end of a procedurally assembled industrial firing yard. Walk/look with WASD/mouse, sprint with Shift, jump with Space, retry with R, mute with M and quit with Escape. A visible fixed tower tracks the player when in range with clear sight, warns before firing, recoils through automatic bursts and recovers before acquiring again. Tracer geometry and muzzle flash show fire. Health, a brief impact overlay/camera impulse, and generated shot/impact/footstep/warning sounds make hits legible. Arm swing and camera motion animate locomotion; turret traverse, barrel movement and recoil animate the threat. These are procedural proxy animations and original synthesized effects, not finished character art or recorded weapons.

The first blocking visibility hit decides damage. The turret cannot damage through cover, behind itself through its own geometry, outside its configured range or into the protected spawn. Loss of sight cancels acquisition/burst and requires a new warning; cadence never catches up with an unbounded backlog after a hitch. The controller accepts explicit simulation delta and sight/range inputs. Reject nonfinite/negative time and invalid configuration. Test acquisition, exact timing boundaries, lost sight, range, hitch bounds, health clamp and duplicate lethal requests deterministically before integration.

The initial build used an instant reset. The accepted [persistent-death continuation](persistent-deaths.md) now replaces it with a short ragdoll camera pull-back/return and retained bodies. World/turret state continues and the slice still saves no run. Retry does not claim a record. Character collision and movement follow Unreal Character/CharacterMovement and the installed First Person C++ template patterns; keep input, presentation, core threat/health rules and procedural scene assembly separate.

The procedural recipe uses fixed dimensions and no random placement. It creates a floor, boundary walls, cover, tower, lighting and signage from engine primitives/materials, with explicit centimeters at the Unreal adapter. Its named scene builder runs once in a fresh world; retries do not duplicate geometry. All assets required by packaging must be explicit hard references or cooked directories. Keep this first slice free of external asset downloads and editor-only runtime dependencies.

Acceptance includes fast real C++ rule tests, a real Unreal build, an integration scenario covering safe spawn, in-range damage, cover, lethal reset and bounded actors, a rendered packaged launch, visual review and audio-output evidence. A silent/no-render process does not establish the animation or sound result. Record exact launch/build/test commands, output location and remaining limits with the executable.
